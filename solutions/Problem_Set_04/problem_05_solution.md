# Problem 5: A Perpendicular Line and Its Intersection

## Problem

Find the equation of the line through $P=(2,1)$ perpendicular to $3x+y-4=0$. Solve the two line equations to find their point of intersection. Give the equation of the new line in slope-intercept form.

## Worked Solution

Rewrite the given line as $y=-3x+4$, so its slope is $-3$. A perpendicular line has slope $1/3$, the negative reciprocal. Through $(2,1)$ it is
$$y-1=\frac13(x-2),\qquad\boxed{y=\frac13x+\frac13}.$$
Its general form is $x-3y-1=0$.

To find the intersection with the original line, substitute $y=(x+1)/3$ into $3x+y-4=0$:
$$3x+\frac{x+1}{3}-4=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad10x=11\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{11}{10}.$$
Then
$$y=\frac13\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)+\frac13=\frac{7}{10}.$$

**Answer:** intersection $\boxed{(11/10,7/10)}$; perpendicular line $\boxed{y=x/3+1/3}$.

**Check.** The intersection satisfies $3x+y=33/10+7/10=4$ and $y=x/3+1/3$.
