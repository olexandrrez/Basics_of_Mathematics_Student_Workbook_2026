# Problem 4: Centre, Vertices, and Asymptotes of a Hyperbola

## Problem

Find the centre, vertices, and asymptotes of
$$\frac{(x-2)^2}{9}-\frac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1.$$

## Worked Solution

Compare with the horizontal hyperbola form
$$\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1.$$
Here $(h,k)=(2,-1)$, $a=3$, and $b=2$. The vertices are displaced by $a$ along the horizontal transverse axis:
$$ (h\pm a,k)=(2\pm3,-1),$$
so they are $\boxed{(-1,-1)}$ and $\boxed{(5,-1)}$.

The asymptote equations are obtained by setting the translated hyperbola's right side to zero:
$$y-k=\pm\frac ba(x-h).$$
Thus
$$\boxed{y+1=\pm\frac23(x-2)}.$$

**Check.** Both asymptotes pass through the centre $(2,-1)$ and have slopes $\pm2/3$, consistent with $b/a=2/3$.
