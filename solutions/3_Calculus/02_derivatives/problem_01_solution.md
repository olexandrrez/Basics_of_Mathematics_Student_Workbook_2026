# Problem 1: Derivative of a Polynomial and the Slope of a Tangent Line

## 1. Original Problem
For $f(x)=3x^5-2x^3+7x-4$, find $f'(x)$, evaluate at 0 and 1, determine the signs, and interpret the tangent slopes.

## 2. What We Are Trying to Find
We need the derivative function and its values at two specified inputs. Those values are instantaneous slopes of the curve, so their signs indicate whether the graph is rising or falling locally.

## 3. Key Idea or Rule Needed Before the Calculation
Use linearity of differentiation and the power rule $(x^n)'=nx^{n-1}$. A constant has derivative zero. At a point $x=a$, $f'(a)$ is the slope of the tangent line.

## 4. Step-by-Step Solution
Differentiate the polynomial term by term:
$$f'(x)=3(5x^4)-2(3x^2)+7-0=15x^4-6x^2+7.$$
At $x=0$, $f'(0)=7$. At $x=1$, $f'(1)=15-6+7=16$. Both values are positive.

## 5. Why Each Step Is Valid
The derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives, and constant coefficients remain as multipliers. Since $7>0$ and $16>0$, both tangent lines rise from left to right. The larger value 16 means the tangent at $x=1$ is steeper than at $x=0$.

## 6. Final Answer in Words
$f'(x)=15x^4-6x^2+7$, with $f'(0)=7$ and $f'(1)=16$. The curve has a positive tangent slope at both points.

## 7. Quick Verification
At $x=0$ all power terms in $f'$ vanish, leaving 7. At $x=1$ direct substitution gives $15-6+7=16$.

## 8. Common Mistakes
- Forgetting to multiply by the exponent in the power rule.
- Keeping the constant $-4$ in the derivative.
- Calling a positive derivative a positive function value; it describes slope, not height.

## 9. Short In-Class Explanation
Differentiate each term using the power rule to get $15x^4-6x^2+7$. Its values at zero and one are 7 and 16. Both are positive, so the tangent lines rise at both points.
